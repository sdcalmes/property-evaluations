## 2. Buyer fit / land-use concept

**A plausible wooded custom-home site, but not yet a proven orchard/garden/workshop site.** Two acres can be plenty, but the usable arrangement matters more than the headline acreage. This is a long narrow strip rather than a broad estate lot. With ordinary 25-ft side offsets, a 150-ft width leaves approximately 100 ft between side building limits; lawful proportional offsets may differ. A house and attached three-car garage, drive, septic disposal/reserve, detached workshop, sunny vegetable garden, orchard, lawn and retained screening need to fit together on a survey.

Kitchen, mudroom, pantry and basement storage could be designed to the buyer's specifications, but are not present assets and receive no existing-home scores. No numerical buyer-fit score is assigned: applying the house-weighted model to mostly unknown future improvements would mislead.

The inspected listing photograph shows a dense leafy woodland edge, mature trunks and substantial undergrowth. This supports screening potential, but not a claim that all 2.28 acres are mature forest, dry, level or usable. Two additional listing photos could not be retrieved. No parcel aerial, terrain model, wetland or floodplain overlay was successfully inspected. Sunlight, grade and clearing needs remain open.

For the buyer's 30×40 vegetable garden, reserve a sunny area wider than that rectangle to account for tree shade, access and fencing. Fruit trees need a separate sunlight and spacing plan. Clearing a building site alone does not establish a good orchard area. Keep the septic field and reserve clear of planned tree roots, structures and vehicle loads according to the approved septic plan.

## 4. Zoning / outbuilding / environmental review

Vernon's current A-3 Suburban Estate rules (§300-32) list two-acre minimum area, 175-ft minimum average width, 50-ft setback, 25-ft offset, 10% maximum total building footprint, 75,000 sq ft open space and 18-ft accessory-building height. **The public description's 150-ft width falls short of today's 175-ft standard.** Section 300-11 recognizes preexisting legal lots of record and certain substandard lots of record as of February 26, 1959. Section 300-9 provides proportional side offsets for some narrow lots. This is a verification issue, not a finding that the lot is unbuildable. Obtain written zoning confirmation identifying the lawful lot status, creation date, applicable setbacks and any needed approvals. Older indexed ordinance text contained additional common-ownership provisions; use the current code and Village interpretation, not an old crawl.

Under §300-14, two-to-three-acre parcels have an accessory-building aggregate limit of the smaller of 1.5% of lot area or 1,960 sq ft. On reported acreage, 99,316.8 × 1.5% = **1,489.8 sq ft**. Thus a **1,200-sq-ft 30×40 workshop appears feasible under the size cap**, leaving only about 290 sq ft for other counted accessory buildings. Confirm treatment of garage arrangements and any excluded structures. Buildings over 900 sq ft require a recorded restriction on commercial/industrial use; detached building placement generally excludes the front yard and requires compatibility with the home. Size compliance does not establish a feasible building location or override private covenants, environmental overlays or septic separation rules. Verify all proposed uses, including fences.

GIS service metadata was reachable but actual parcel queries were inaccessible in this environment (direct county access returned 403). No claim is made that wetlands, floodplain, environmental corridor or high groundwater are absent. Vernon code also imposes wetland offsets. A mapped wetland screen would still not replace a delineation. Obtain parcel geometry, current zoning map, DNR wetlands/floodplain, environmental corridor/high-groundwater overlays, contours and a site walk after rain. Neither woods nor a residential zoning label proves dry soils. Natural acreage can add privacy/wildlife even where building or clearing is limited.

A long lot may provide a pleasant wooded rear retreat, but adjacent vacant land has already traded. Do not assume its trees will remain. Verify rear and side ownership, zoning and pending development. No protected common land or conservation easement was identified; shared acreage is not included in the 2.28-acre figure.

## 6. Taxes / development costs

The approximately $1,085 annual raw-land tax is not the future tax on a completed home. Relative to $177,000 it is roughly 0.61%, but that ratio is not a reliable future tax rate. Municipal assessment is not market value; obtain the actual bill, credits/special charges, equalized value and assessor guidance for the completed project.

No current contractor quotes were obtained. Obtain separate written budgets for survey/soils/design/permits, clearing/stumps, grading/drainage, driveway/culvert, utility extensions, well, septic including any mound, foundation excavation, landscaping, detached shop and contingency. Unknown water/sewer does not mean a tested septic site. Nearby homes use private well/septic, but that is not proof of the subject's approved system type.

At an illustrative $180,000 land cost, the buyer's $900,000 total ceiling leaves **$720,000 for everything else**. Pure arithmetic scenarios: $100,000 other non-house costs leave $620,000 for the home; $150,000 leave $570,000; $200,000 leave $520,000. These are budget sensitivities, not construction estimates. A roughly 3,500-sq-ft two-story plus finished basement, three-car garage, workshop and developed landscape requires a builder feasibility budget before buying land. Do not confuse affordable land with an affordable finished project.

## 8. Verdict

**The July price looks sensible, but this is not a currently verified available lot.** If it comes back near $180,000 with written buildability and a workable site plan, it is worth investigating. Strong points are the wooded setting, enough nominal acreage for privacy and outdoor projects, potentially compatible workshop allowance and freeway access. Main reservations are its narrow form, unverified soils/environmental constraints, clearing/sunlight tradeoff, family-drive geography and the cost of delivering the entire dream build. Neighbor's $185,000 sale supports the price neighborhood; it does not remove those risks.
