### Garage entry / mudroom — provisional 8.0/10
Published model-home descriptions/photo captions call out a **rear foyer/drop zone**, distinct from the dramatic front foyer. Confirm that the actual garage-to-house path goes through it, flooring, coat/shoe storage, and whether it crowds laundry.

### Garage / shed / outbuilding — 8.5/10 for current garage, weak extra-building flexibility
Three-car attached garage fits current storage better than a two-car. Obtain **inside dimensions**, depth for vehicles, power, solar inverter location and any storage restrictions. Village of Sussex Ch. 17 §17.0704 addresses accessory garages and buildings: standard residential accessory **shed** limits include permit, **250 sq ft**, 15-ft height, approximately 10-ft separation from main structure and 5-ft lot-line setback (district and amendments must be confirmed); a second detached **garage** with an already attached garage should **not** be presumed allowable. HOA may be stricter; require written Village zoning and HOA determinations. A **30×40-ft workshop (1,200 sq ft) is not a supported assumption** here.

### Basement utility/storage — provisional 8.3/10
Full **unfinished** poured-concrete lower level, bathroom stub, sump, radon mitigation: good immediate storage and potential finishability, **but no finished lower level is included in today's price comparison**. Check slab moisture, radon test results, ceiling height, egress and rough-in location. Confirm high-efficiency mechanicals and solar interfaces do not crowd storage. Finishing ~800–1,200 sq ft can cost materially extra; no specific bid obtained.

### Garden / outdoor — provisional 6.0/10
**0.40 acres does not automatically equal a legal, sunny 30×40 garden location.** This is a ~1,200-sq-ft footprint vs ~17,424 gross land sq ft, before house, driveway, setbacks, drainage, park adjacency, solar needs, possible easements, pool paths, landscape, and HOA rules. Request recorded plat/survey, architectural guidelines, written HOA permission for deer fencing/raised beds/orchard and aerial sun exposure. Garden and young-child play area may be possible, but privacy, mature trees and personal natural habitat are substantially below the user's larger-lot ideal.
