## 4. Lot, HOA and restrictions

The exclusive parcel is approximately **1.01 acres**. The [HOA site map](https://woodlandridgehoamerton.com/association-documents) depicts subdivision natural-resource area and Village-dedicated park, which could provide durable green buffering, but it does not prove which tract adjoins the subject or that the whole wooded backdrop is protected. Neighboring Woodland Ridge records sometimes describe an interest in an outlot; obtain this parcel's deed and title commitment to establish any undivided interest and obligations. Do not count common land as exclusive acreage. The site map is a marketing plat rather than a parcel survey or environmental delineation.

The August master-database review of the [HOA declaration](https://woodlandridgehoamerton.com/association-documents) recorded **no accessory structures or sheds of any kind**, architectural approval for gardens and fences, a general four-foot fence cap, and no chain-link. This is unusually consequential for this buyer: a three-car garage must carry shed storage, and a four-foot fence may not keep deer out of a vegetable garden. The declaration PDF could not be independently retrieved in this session; obtain the recorded declaration, amendments, current ACC rules and written HOA guidance for a proposed fenced garden before offering. Verify whether raised beds, deer netting and play equipment have separate rules. The earlier Sheet review noted an 84-lot budget with roughly $19,000 assets/reserves and no 2026 reserve contribution budgeted; reconfirm from current financial statements and minutes.

The mound system and its reserve area, drainage/utility easements, setbacks and tree shade can shrink the practical garden/buildable area. A parcel-specific overlay of mapped wetlands, floodplain and contours was **not resolved**; the report makes no parcel-level clearance claim. Mapped wetland, if present, would not substitute for a surveyed delineation. Obtain survey/plat, septic as-built and county GIS layers before assuming fencing, grading or new improvements are feasible. The Village zoning and HOA may independently restrict an outbuilding, and the HOA restriction is currently the limiting concern.

The defining tradeoff is an acre in an HOA that apparently forbids sheds and controls garden/fence design. Privacy is attractive visually but its legal durability remains unproven. Provisional buyer fit **7.35/10** from the repo scoring helper with uncertain items rated provisionally, separate from market value.

At the unchanged $799,900 ask after roughly a month, a defensible **opening offer is $780,000–$790,000** with normal inspection and document contingencies. **$790,000–$810,000** is a reasonable purchase zone if the rear land, usable garden space, HOA answer and inspections are favorable. **$820,000** is a caution price; **around $830,000** is a walk-away reference under current evidence, not an invitation to bid there. Tight competition could affect tactics, but enthusiasm should not substitute for these verifications. A written garden/fence denial, a poor septic report, or development exposure behind the lot warrants a lower range or passing.
