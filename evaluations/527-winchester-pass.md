## 2. Buyer-fit review

### Kitchen — 6.5/10; pantry — 7/10

The approximately 10 ft 3 in × 14 ft 10 in kitchen has a compact island with seating, useful windows, substantial oak cabinetry and a tall full-height pantry cabinet. That cabinet is useful dedicated food storage, but there is no verified walk-in pantry. Adjacent dining measures approximately 8 ft 7 in × 14 ft 10 in. Black appliances, apparent laminate counters and traditional oak finishes make this a functional but dated kitchen rather than a renovated premium one. Counter material is a photo inference.

Test two-cook aisle clearance with the dishwasher and refrigerator open, landing space beside the range, island stool interference, exhaust discharge and appliance condition. A cosmetic refresh may be sufficient if the layout feels good; do not buy assuming an inexpensive remodel will solve a layout you dislike.

### Garage entry / mudroom — 6/10

The garage enters a small hall beside an approximately 8 ft 1 in × 5 ft 7 in laundry. Laundry cabinetry and hooks help, but the photographs/plan do not establish a substantial dedicated drop zone with bench and individual lockers. Walk the school-bag, boot and laundry path with doors open. This is adequate circulation with limited containment for family clutter.

### Garage / shed — 7.5/10, provisional

Three stalls satisfy the basic vehicle-count target, but about 608 sq ft is compact once three vehicles, bicycles, mower and tools are present. Photos show a double and single opening, exposed studs/roof structure and garden equipment. Verify which plan dimension is clear width/depth, door widths, SUV clearance, steps and actual usable wall space. Bring vehicle dimensions and a proposed storage layout. No separate shed was identified in the 61-image gallery; existence is unconfirmed.

The developer's [Winchester Fields declaration PDF](https://jbjcompanies.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/Winchester-Fields-DR.pdf), section 6.4(c), describes **129–180 sq ft rectangular, non-square sheds matching the residence**, subject to architectural approval. It excludes steel, vinyl, aluminum, prefabricated and kit structures. A custom 10×14 wood shed could be a candidate; an off-the-shelf resin shed should not be assumed acceptable. Section 6.4(b) provides an approval path for certain detached garages, but no extra workshop is required or separately penalized in this evaluation.

Important scope limit: the subject is Phase IV. The recovered PDF includes the base declaration and amendment material, but the exact Phase IV annexation and all currently recorded amendments were not verified. Treat these provisions as a serious diligence flag until title and the current association confirm applicability and approvals. Current small-shed permission remains unknown.

The [Village small-shed guide](https://www.richfieldwi.gov/DocumentCenter/View/3586) separately describes a permit/survey process for sheds up to 192 sq ft, rear/side placement, typical 10-ft building/lot-line separation, height and well/septic clearances. District rules may be stricter. Municipal allowance does not override private restrictions. Septic tank, field, reserve, well and easements must be located first.

### Basement utility/storage — 9/10, subject to moisture inspection

The large unfinished basement provides substantial storage without paying for finished recreation space. Exposed masonry, joists and small windows support zero finished-area credit. Loose carpet does not make it finished living area. Darker/discolored block at the rear-left lower corner in photo 41 needs explanation and moisture testing. It is not proof of an active leak or structural failure. Inspect during/after rain if possible, review disclosure, sump/drain-tile operation, wall movement, grading and downspout discharge. A material moisture defect would change both score and value.

### Garden / outdoors — 6/10 pending fence approval

The open southwest/rear lawn appears to offer sun, play space and a physical 30×40-ft garden candidate. The parcel study places a 40×30 rectangle inside the mapped boundary without building overlap. This proves only mapped geometry: tree shade, actual grade, septic reserve and legal placement remain unverified. The contour pattern suggests roughly 2 ft over a 30-ft local run in this area; it is not a flat surveyed pad. Walk runoff after rain and assess whether raised beds or modest leveling are needed.

**Fence permission is the largest garden risk.** Declaration section 6.5(p) restricts front-setback fences and wire-mesh fences, with an architectural-approval exception described for dog runs. Do not infer a garden exemption. Seek a written decision on the exact deer-fence design/material/height before removing contingencies. Garden use itself is not established as prohibited; a practical fence may be difficult to approve.

### Other fit

Lot/privacy 7/10: mature tree edges and generous lawn, with neighboring homes visible; no verified protected natural-area backing. Condition/capital exposure 6.5/10 reflects unknown system ages and inspection needs. Resale 7.5/10: main-floor primary, three-car garage, acreage and three baths help; only three enclosed bedrooms, dated finishes and bedroom separation across floors narrow some family appeal. Taxes 9/10 uses the known historical bill, with revaluation uncertainty. Location 6.5/10 balances the longer daily work trip against better access to Sam’s parents. **Overall experimental fit 7.04/10, 100% weight coverage**. This score does not determine market value.
