## 2. Buyer-fit review

**Kitchen: 8/10 provisional.** Photos 19, 20 and 24 show a large stone-look island, seating, extensive white perimeter cabinets, contrasting wood island base, substantial counter space, a range/hood and patterned backsplash. Beams and skylights add character and daylight. The listed 18.5×14 footprint is promising for cooking. The refrigerator remains a conspicuous white unit and upper wall/wood finishes are retained. This is a meaningful kitchen update within an older house, not evidence of a full-house renovation. Verify cabinet construction, island clearances, drawers, pantry depth and exterior exhaust venting. It looks functionally stronger than a small cosmetic kitchen refresh.

**Garage entry / mudroom: not scored.** The 11×7.5 main-floor utility/laundry room has a sink, washer/dryer and shower. It is not visibly a purpose-built coat/boot drop zone. Garage access to the basement could mean carrying groceries or a child upstairs from some bays. Walk each garage-to-kitchen path and identify where coats, bags, shoes and stroller would actually go. A spacious foyer at the front does not prove mudroom function off the garage.

**Garage / outbuilding: 9/10 provisional, based on listing capacity.** Five claimed spaces are a substantial strength, potentially meeting storage/workshop needs without building anew. No inspected photo establishes bay dimensions, door height, independence of bays, heat/electrical service or fire separation. Do not assume “five-car attached” means five convenient side-by-side main-floor bays. Future 30×40 building potential is plausible under broad area rules, but the mapped natural-resource area, setbacks, grade, septic reserve and title restrictions must be checked first.

**Basement storage: unscored.** The 1,424 finished sqft includes rec space, two bedrooms and a full bath. Photos show extensive paneling, carpet and suspended ceilings; remaining unfinished storage and mechanical footprint are unknown. Confirm storage separately from garage capacity and check for moisture behind finished walls.

**Garden: 8/10 provisional.** Yard photos 54 and 57 show a substantial open lawn surrounded by trees, visibly more useful than four acres of dense forest alone. A 30×40 garden appears physically plausible, but no measured rectangle has been established. Check summer sunlight, drainage, deer-fence rights and septic field/reserve location. Mature canopy creates excellent privacy and shade but reduces sunny orchard/meadow space. Avoid pricing the whole parcel as sunny, level, fenceable land.

**Lot/privacy: 9.5/10 provisional.** Tree cover, screening and the rear lawn are strong matches for privacy, wildlife, family play and sitting outside. Drone photo 64 shows neighboring homes/lawns at the edges, so this is a spacious established neighborhood rather than an isolated rural estate. Leaf-off privacy and road/neighbor sound still need a visit.

Other provisional ratings: location 5.5, resale 7, taxes 7, condition/capital exposure 5, pantry 6.5. Missing mudroom and basement-storage ratings are excluded and weights renormalized. The resulting buyer-fit score is a comparison aid and does not determine the value or offer. Bedroom placement is an additional family tradeoff: primary and one child can share the main level today, but further children or guests may need downstairs bedrooms.

## 4. Photo / condition analysis

Exterior appears cared for with wood siding, established landscaping, roof planes over multiple sections, brick entry piers, extensive windows and a large A-frame glazed room. The front steps and foyer stairwell matter when carrying Leila, groceries or a stroller. Photos cannot establish siding rot, roof age, flashing or structural condition. Main living spaces have vaulted ceilings, exposed wood, a fireplace and leafy views. There is good daylight near the glazed rooms, but canopy and heavy wood finishes will make some rooms darker in winter/evening. The sunken living room creates an internal step. New-looking flooring and an updated kitchen coexist with older doors, trim and paneling.

Bath condition is mixed. Photo 32 shows a modern glass/tiled shower. The laundry shower has older enclosure/cabinetry. Lower bath photos show a distinctive blue freestanding tub, brick surround, pedestal sink, plaid trim and tile; this is an older aesthetic and a potential remodel, not current luxury. Outside, a broad paver patio/firepit area, terraced steps and mowed lawn are real lifestyle positives. Check settlement, retaining-wall movement and surface-water flow toward the house. The A-frame space is especially attractive, but extensive glazing/skylights carry leak and future replacement exposure. Confirm which enclosed rooms are conditioned and included in above-grade living area versus seasonal porches.

**Renovation interpretation:** meaningful kitchen and shower work plus selective flooring, with substantial retained vintage fabric. No recent-sale evidence suggests a quick flip. Cosmetic updates can wait if you like the wood; major-system checks cannot be substituted with attractive photos.

## 5. Lot / GIS / environmental

Official city parcel query confirmed Lot 13, parcel ID, acreage and assessment. Mapped boundary is irregular with a long curving road edge to the east and narrower north/south boundaries. Parcel GIS is not a survey. Recorded assessor plat is **Assessor's Plat of The Ridges, document 1115262**, with city layer Lazer_ID **158276**; obtain the actual recorded plat and deed. There is no evidence of shared acres in the 4.02 figure, but title interests/easements remain unreviewed.

City environmental screen used the **bounding envelope enclosing the entire parcel**. It returned zero intersecting Wetland Areas (Environmental MapServer/8), zero 2020 Flood Hazard Areas (Flood_Zones_2023/1) and zero features in the city's mapped Easements layer. This is encouraging mapped screening only. An empty easement layer is not clear title, and absent mapped wetlands is not a field delineation. No independent DNR field/soil determination was obtained. Map dates/data completeness may vary.

Zoning query returned **RCE — Country Estate District**. Environmental Corridors query returned **INRA** and **OUT**, including a follow-up query using a simplified parcel outline. INRA means isolated natural resource area; OUT represents land outside the mapped resource areas. Therefore some natural-resource mapping appears to intersect the property, but exact acreage and proposed-building impacts were not measured. Regional classification does not itself prove a recorded conservation easement or a particular city permit restriction. Ask Muskego planning for a written overlay/clearing/building interpretation; do not automatically apply county town-zoning rules to this city parcel.

Contours were available. The broader parcel bounding envelope intersects elevations roughly 907–979 feet; these include adjacent land and **must not be described as the exact parcel relief**. Photos independently show meaningful terrain changes and terraced landscaping. A site visit/survey is necessary to distinguish usable lawn from grading-intensive areas. Drainage paths, septic reserve, steep slopes and retaining walls should guide garden/play/building placement.

**30×40 outbuilding:** current city code §400-167 gives large residential parcels a total detached-accessory area allowance up to 2% of parcel area, subject to other rules. On 4.02 recorded acres that calculation is approximately **3,502 sqft total**, not a guaranteed new-building entitlement. Attached structures are treated as part of the principal building for regulatory purposes. RCE base requirements include 50-foot street setback and 40-foot other offsets. Number of accessory structures, architecture, height, existing detached floor area, septic reserve, grade and natural-resource protections still apply. Get a city-reviewed concept location plus title clearance before paying a premium for this possibility. An existing five-space garage may satisfy much of the need already.

A nearby historic Stonecrest listing in The Ridges claimed no association fees or neighborhood restrictions. This makes HOA absence plausible, but does not establish current covenants for Lot 13. Online HOAs named “The Ridges” in Nebraska and other states were excluded. Ask for seller disclosure, deed/title Schedule B-II, plat notes and any restrictions. Check deer fencing, gardens, sheds, recreational trails and any wildlife/hunting use specifically. No hunting right or protected future-development buffer is established.

## 9. Offer strategy / verdict

- **Opening:** approximately **$735,000–$745,000** with appropriate inspection/well/septic/title protections; choose upper end if a tour resolves layout and systems favorably.
- **Reasonable purchase target:** **$755,000–$780,000** assuming sound major systems and acceptable rights.
- **Caution:** approximately **$790,000**, requiring strong documentation and especially good in-person condition.
- **Approximate walk-away:** **$800,000** on current evidence; move lower for material repairs/restricted utility, not upward simply because the setting is appealing.

The relisting and serial cuts favor measured negotiation. A fresh $799,900 cut may draw buyers, so confirm competition rather than assuming desperation. The advertised concession has no known dollar value; do not double-count an assumed credit in an offer. These bands are judgment ranges rather than appraisal precision.

What I like most is the **combination of privacy, usable open lawn, a substantial kitchen and abundant claimed garage capacity**. What concerns me most is **unknown oil/septic/roof exposure, only two main-level bedrooms, unverified mudroom/storage function and the longer trip to Lisa's Mom**. This is worth touring if you like the wood/vaulted-room aesthetic. It is a weaker match if the goal is a large conventional two-story family layout with bedrooms grouped together and an obvious mudroom. At full ask, you should be buying verified condition and usable rights, not just a beautiful four-acre description.
